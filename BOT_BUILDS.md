# Survivor bots

Ultra treats primary fire as the ongoing attack, with available skills woven around it. Target aim updates even before the firing gate opens: a native movement or unavailable-utility driver cannot leave hitscan aim pointing along a strafe. Native readiness, interrupt priority, state-machine sharing and held/charged input rules determine which attacks can overlap. Walking is separate from sprint cancellation; aiming a weapon does not itself stop pursuit. Native movement utilities and unknown busy states retain movement ownership. Incoming attacks release the reservation on a native escape instead of indefinitely blocking its last stock.

Combat routes exclude the standing start node, retain a short commitment, remember recently reached points and track actual body displacement independently of changing route goals. Native broad navigation handles intermediate waypoints. Bounded direct steps bridge a temporarily idle navigator or a reached firing position, with collision, ground and objective checks. Dodges combine lateral motion with continued route progress. Dodge checks are cached for 0.1 seconds; direct-step checks for 0.15 seconds. Route discovery remains bounded and bot decisions/grants retain the shared round-robin budget.

Local recovery also measures progress along the current route, so sideways A/D movement cannot disguise a stall. It tests actual collision normals, selects a supported wall tangent or another direction, then briefly commits rather than reversing every frame. Recovery probes are cached for 0.15 seconds and capped at four bot probes per rendered frame, with at most three candidate directions per probe. Recovery uses ordinary movement and validated jump inputs; it cannot teleport through geometry.

The host uses every living hostile's position for Ultra's pursuit decisions, including distant and stealthed bodies. It searches the native ground graph in slices and follows connected legs around obstacles, checking hull restrictions, gates, jump reach and progress. Unreachable targets are temporarily deprioritized. The local combat planner takes over at fighting distance. Ultra can navigate toward a hidden position, and it needs native visibility and a clear ray before firing.

In group fights, Ultra ranks available shots separately from danger. The route scorer checks whether a firing position is exposed to other attackers and favors cover that blocks those sightlines. A credible lethal cast or severe recent damage can trigger a brief cover retreat. With room to fight, Ultra keeps attacking as it repositions. On KOTH, it prioritizes reaching and holding the zone.

Ultra's build scorer evaluates defensive investment against the rest of its inventory. Missing protection and sustain compete with damage choices, and repeated small hits or defeats raise that priority even at full health after respawning. Evidence decays and diminishing returns preserve offensive choices. Shield-only builds avoid ineffective health sustain, void conversion costs remain in force, and all choices come from the actual host-created offer. No stats, stock, cooldowns, damage or movement speeds are granted outside the ordinary rules.

Low, Medium and High use survivor-appropriate priority plans. Extra High and Ultra score their current inventory, survivor role, health/shields, crit, proc synergies and available pickups instead of following a fixed item list. Unknown custom items use their catalog tags; this is a heuristic, not a simulation of arbitrary mod scripts.

| Fixed plan | Main goals |
|---|---|
| On-hit | Syringes, daggers, missiles, lightning, shield support and later proc upgrades. |
| Critical | Glasses, Predatory Instincts, Harvester's Scythe, Shatterspleen and Laser Scope. |
| Skill caster | Backup Magazines, Kjaro's/Runald's Bands, Alien Head, Afterburner and Lysate Cell. |
| Bulwark | Block, armor, healing, health and nearby damage for sustained fights. |

Fixed plans select consistent ordinary/void routes per bot. They do not buy a void item that corrupts owned setup pieces, nor buy ordinary items already converted by a held void item. Caster plans retain ordinary bands instead of later destroying them with Singularity Band. Crit plans preserve their normal crit/bleed dependencies. Plasma Shrimp builds prioritize missing shield support; full-shield builds avoid health-only healing goals. Purity is not a default fixed goal alongside luck/crit/proc investments.

Ultra reads the visible items and equipment in up to three opposing inventories, prioritizing nearby opponents and recent encounters. Inventories are cached per master for half a second. It tracks each opponent's rushing, strafing, recovery and damage trades during the match. It also uses the bot's equipped skills and the damage those skills actually land to update its own build priorities. An offered level competes with items based on its value to the current build. For example, Ultra can buy multi-hit pressure against Safer Spaces, slows against a frequent strafer, or a Kinetic Dampener against heavy burst. Catalog eligibility, expansion settings, and host bans and caps apply to every choice.

All tiers pursue and remain inside a ready KOTH capture area. Low has simpler aim and target selection. Medium keeps the earlier aim parameters and target scoring. High sees nearby opposing summons as combat targets. Ultra's own skill range and its experience trading damage determine whether to close in, hold an angle, or retreat. It can route toward a hidden opponent's position. New attacks require line of sight and a short reaction window. Known projectile skills use bounded interception, while other skills use native aim.

Ultra looks for reachable firing angles and cover on the map's ground graph. It tries another corridor after sustained movement stalls and biases hill positions away from teammates. After a bad trade, it stays in retreat briefly when cover breaks sight. A visible native attack recovering can suggest a short opening. The estimate uses the skill's base recharge and observed recasts, while Ultra checks its own readiness before pushing. Supported native escapes stay in reserve during risky engagements. Repeated rushes, strafing, recovery, and burst damage influence later builds, and those observations decay as opponents change tactics. Bot travel uses the native position-based path API. A bare waypoint has no hurtbox, so the target-based overload previously discarded it. The travel fallback has no attack skill and follows the native drivers in their original order. Registered bots opt into native cliff detection; extra ground probes run at most ten times per second per bot, with safe lateral alternatives. Validated graph jumps and in-flight utilities retain native handling. These checks prevent deliberate blind walking; they do not guarantee immunity to knockback, failed abilities or malformed navigation in custom maps.

Tactical and item-choice work share a round-robin, 2 ms soft budget per server physics update. Native AI/physics/pathfinding retain their own game costs. One indivisible physics query or native inventory callback can exceed the soft budget. Reward offers are rolled immediately on the host, then scored and consumed under this budget, including death choices without a surviving body. A bad offer stays banked with its original IDs/class while other offers can be considered. The 12-offer cap, catch-up accounting and host validation remain in force.

Extra High sidesteps and jumps on a staggered cadence. Ultra reacts to visible attack states and tracks projectiles on a collision course. Both use native sprint and skill recovery. Combat jumps use actual jump power and prospective sprint speed to test overhead clearance, route and landing support. Ultra uses the same grounded movement inputs as a player, and each survivor's existing skills retain their native movement.

Ultra keeps a model for each equipped skill definition and activation state, refreshing it when a loadout or stance changes. Registered state configuration supplies numeric priors; public numeric fields on an already-running state and real bullet, projectile, melee and blast emissions refine them. Damage coefficient, proc coefficient, hit count, range, speed, falloff, duration, charge time, control and healing affect available-attack scoring. Direct authoritative hits refine proc evidence and cast success. Item proc chains are excluded from base-weapon observations. Skill data is indexed 24 configurations per update, and at most four participant models are sampled per update, each no faster than every 0.12 seconds. No discovery casts, state instantiation, assembly scans or asset loads are performed.

The controller evaluates the four slots instead of following a survivor-name combo list. It values proc-heavy attacks with proc items, known kill opportunities, control against pressure and healing while wounded. Known physical range can exceed a native AI driver's preferred firing distance; loadout exclusions and actual readiness still apply. Confirmed charged primaries and secondaries hold for their learned duration before releasing normally. Scope/reload, deployment, traversal and other complex native input chains retain native handling. Primary fire continues during independent skill recovery and waits when a shared state machine genuinely owns the weapon.

Opponent models record movement, strafing, jumping, sprinting, skill variety and cast/hit outcomes. A rolling confidence estimate gradually adjusts Ultra's reaction, aim damping and evasion to the human it is fighting; briefly standing still does not reset it. This does not impose artificial attack pauses. An aimed active skill's estimated damage accounts for armor, shields, barrier, expected crit and the host damage multiplier. Observed single-stock recovery can suggest an opening; multistock skills are not assumed empty after one cast. Skill burst/proc/DoT evidence also feeds the existing defensive item scorer. Learning is local to the server and current match, survives body respawns and resets with the run.

Unexposed custom logic, unusual skill protocols and effects not represented in observed attacks remain estimates. The model does not understand arbitrary scripts or guarantee a counter to every build. Native cooldowns, input rules, navigation meshes and multiplayer authority still apply.

No AI or bot-item logic runs on joining clients. Clients receive the normal replicated masters, inventories and actions. Starting counts remain mode-specific; intelligence, decision interval and Chase are shared host settings. No optional mod or external AI service is required.
